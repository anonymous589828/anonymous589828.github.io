MIDI Transcription and Loudness Estimation from Clarinet Recordings Using Contact Microphones

Abstract

We present a method for analyzing individual clarinet performances in a wind orchestra using contact microphones. The goal is to precisely quantify performance features (e.g., pitch, onset/offset time, and loudness) from individual players' recordings, for practice support. While previous studies have used high directional microphones to reduce sound contamination, this approach is less effective when players are distributed in two dimensions. As an alternative, we use contact microphones, which significantly reduce interference from other players, with drawbacks that they catch noise from key movements and have acoustical characteristics that differ from perceived sounds. To estimate the performance features from contact-microphone recordings using deep neural networks, we collect synchronized recordings using both contact and condenser microphones and propose transfer learning methods that do not require annotated training data. Experiments show that the proposed method achieves high accuracy in transcribing note timings and pitch and in estimating loudness. We also demonstrate its potential in analyzing performance features in clarinet unison performances. The method is applicable to a wide variety of musical instruments.
